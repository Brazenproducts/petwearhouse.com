What to Look For in a Dog Seat Cover

Waterproofing Is Non-Negotiable

If a seat cover isn't waterproof, it's a blanket. And blankets don't protect your seats from a wet dog after a lake swim or a muddy trail run. Look for covers with a dedicated waterproof backing layer — not just "water-resistant" fabric, which will eventually soak through.

Hammock vs. Bench

Hammock-style covers create a barrier between the front and back seats, preventing your dog from falling into the footwell during sudden stops. They also block your dog from climbing into the front seat. Bench-style covers just protect the seat surface. For most dog owners, hammock is the way to go — the added safety and containment is worth it.

Non-Slip Backing

A seat cover that slides around every time your dog moves defeats the purpose. Look for rubber or silicone backing that grips the seat surface. Headrest straps and seat anchors help too, but the backing material is what keeps things stable during actual use.

Machine Washable

You will need to wash your seat cover. Frequently. If it's not machine washable, you'll end up hand-scrubbing it in the driveway or just living with a dirty cover. Every option on our list is machine washable because we consider it a baseline requirement.

Side Protection

Dogs scratch door panels. It's not malicious — they're just bracing themselves during turns or trying to look out the window. Side flaps that extend onto the doors protect the areas that seat covers typically miss. The wider the flaps, the better.
