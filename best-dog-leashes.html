What to Look For in a Dog Leash

Length Matters More Than You Think

A 6-foot leash is the standard, and it works for most situations. But if you're walking in crowded areas, near traffic, or working on heel training, a short traffic lead (like the 18-inch BarkTact) gives you dramatically more control. Many experienced handlers keep both — a traffic lead for high-stimulus environments and a standard leash for casual walks.

Material and Hardware

Nylon is the most common leash material, and it's fine for most dogs. But if your dog is a serious puller or you need something that'll survive years of outdoor abuse, look for polyester webbing (higher UV resistance) or biothane (waterproof, doesn't hold odor). For hardware, stainless steel or aluminum clips beat zinc alloy every time — zinc corrodes and weakens faster than you'd expect.

Handle Comfort

If you walk your dog daily (and you should), a padded handle isn't a luxury — it's a necessity. Neoprene padding is the gold standard. Some leashes also offer a secondary traffic handle closer to the clip, which is incredibly useful for quick control in unexpected situations.

Reflective Elements

If you walk your dog at dawn, dusk, or after dark, reflective stitching or tape on your leash is a simple safety upgrade that costs nothing extra on most quality leashes. Don't skip it.

The Bottom Line

A leash is the most-used piece of gear you'll ever buy for your dog. Spend the extra few dollars on something that's built to last, comfortable to hold, and appropriate for your dog's size and behavior.
