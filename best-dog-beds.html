Dogs sleep 12-14 hours a day. Senior dogs even more. That's a lot of time spent on whatever surface they've claimed as their bed — which, if you haven't invested in a proper one, is probably your couch, your actual bed, or a sad flat pillow that lost its shape three months ago.

A good dog bed isn't a luxury. For large breeds and senior dogs, orthopedic support can prevent joint problems and ease existing pain. For destructive chewers, a chew-proof bed saves you from buying a new one every month. And for outdoor dogs, an elevated bed keeps them off hot or cold ground.

We looked at beds across every category and price point. Here are the five that stood out.

How to Choose the Right Dog Bed

Size Matters

Your dog should be able to stretch out fully on their bed without hanging off the edges. Measure your dog from nose to tail while they're lying down and add a few inches. When in doubt, go bigger — dogs don't complain about having too much space.

Orthopedic Support

If your dog is over 50 lbs, over 7 years old, or has any joint issues, orthopedic foam is worth the investment. Look for beds with at least 4 inches of foam — anything less will compress under a large dog's weight and lose its supportive properties. The Big Barker's 7 inches is the gold standard.

Chew-Proofing

Some dogs destroy beds. It's not a training issue — it's just what they do. If your dog is a chewer, skip the plush beds entirely and go straight to an elevated bed with ballistic fabric. You'll save money in the long run by not replacing beds every few weeks.

Washability

Dogs are gross. Lovably gross, but gross. Any bed you buy should have a removable, machine-washable cover at minimum. Elevated beds are even easier — just hose them down. If a bed can't be easily cleaned, it's going to smell terrible within a month.

Indoor vs. Outdoor

Indoor beds prioritize comfort and aesthetics. Outdoor beds prioritize weather resistance and durability. If your dog splits time between both, consider getting one of each — a plush orthopedic bed for inside and a Coolaroo for the patio. Your dog (and your nose) will thank you.

Our Verdict

The Big Barker is the best dog bed money can buy for large breeds — the 10-year guarantee alone makes it worth the premium. For chewers, Kuranda is the only bed that'll survive. For budget shoppers, the FurHaven cooling gel bed punches well above its price. And for outdoor use, the Coolaroo is unbeatable value.
